09. DETERMINISTIC ELECTRICAL CALCULATION ENGINE

Create independently testable pure functions with explicit inputs, units, methods and numerical tolerances. Keep DOM, storage, network, AI and implicit current time outside the engine. Return structured results and diagnostics rather than only formatted strings.

Keep these quantities distinct everywhere:
1. Installed/nameplate capacity.
2. On-state operating demand.
3. Coincident maximum design demand.
4. Starting/inrush demand and duration.
5. Interval-average demand.
6. Time-integrated energy.

An energy duty fraction must never silently reduce the on-state current used for cable, device or starting assessment. An assumed utilization reduction must state its purpose and applicability. Define coincidence, diversity, utilization, growth and spare capacity separately. Show where each factor applies. Do not multiply two reductions that describe the same scheduling effect.

Implement verified formulas with applicability stated:
- DC: P = V × I.
- Single-phase AC electrical input: P = V × I × PF.
- Balanced three-phase AC electrical input: P = √3 × VLL × I × PF.
- Mechanical-output motor entry: Pin = Pout / η.
- Time-integrated energy: E = Σ(Pinterval × actual elapsed Δt).
- Consuming sinusoidal load with P ≥ 0 and displacement-PF magnitude 0 < PFdisp ≤ 1: Q = sQ × P × tan(acos(PFdisp)), with sQ=+1 for inductive absorption and sQ=−1 for capacitive supply under the declared passive sign convention; S = √(P² + Q²).
- Source/regenerative quadrants use explicitly signed P/Q or another verified four-quadrant model. Do not infer Q direction from P's sign. Handle reactive-only elements through a separate supported path.
- Current/PF derived from nameplate kVA or current must not receive a second efficiency adjustment.

For compatible sinusoidal loads at a common electrical boundary, sum real and signed reactive power before calculating the net complex-power magnitude. Preserve leading/lagging cancellation. Keep per-phase currents and equipment loading separate: net reactive cancellation does not erase phase imbalance, branch current or conductor heating.

True PF can include distortion; displacement PF describes fundamental phase shift. Do not derive a phase angle, harmonic spectrum or reactive power from true PF alone when the simple sinusoidal assumptions are not valid. Support additional models only with the necessary measurements and independently verified equations.

Each result exposes: input values and source labels; normalized units; selected model and conditions; formula; intermediate values; rounding rule; assumptions; warnings; source/dataset IDs; project revision; engine version; timestamp; and completeness. Build an Explain result drawer used consistently by KPI cards, tools, plots, warnings and reports.

12. CABLES, VOLTAGE DROP AND LOSSES

Provide independent workflows for voltage drop, current-carrying capacity, protective-device assessment and combined circuit review. A voltage-drop calculation alone is never presented as a complete cable selection.

Collect conductor material, cross-section, length basis, insulation, installation method, loaded-conductor count, ambient/soil conditions, grouping, temperature assumptions and parallel paths where supported. Accept manufacturer resistance/reactance data or a documented resistance model. Preserve the conditions at which values are valid.

For a supported balanced/common single-phase model using per-length impedance and one-way route length L:
- Single-phase two-conductor approximation: ΔV = 2 × I × L × (R cosφ + X sinφ).
- Balanced three-phase approximation: ΔV = √3 × I × L × (R cosφ + X sinφ).
- DC uses the complete outgoing/return path resistance.
Use consistent length units. Handle leading reactive behavior and voltage rise within the stated model. More complex circuits require their own supported calculation, not an unexplained reuse of these expressions.

The two-conductor approximation assumes equal outgoing/return per-length impedance; otherwise use the separately entered path impedances. For every endpoint, assess cumulative source-to-load voltage drop through upstream feeders and the final circuit under the same operating/start scenario. Show segment contributions and the total. Passing the final branch alone does not establish that the complete route meets its limit.

Show volts, percentage, receiving-end estimate, conductor losses, maximum route length for an entered limit and sensitivity to conductor size/temperature. Distinguish running and starting conditions. Use the correct voltage basis for each percentage and clearly identify one-way versus loop length.

Limits come from the selected validated jurisdiction/circuit ruleset or an explicitly entered design criterion. Do not impose a universal 5% rule. Current-carrying capacity requires a sourced base rating and applicable correction factors; every factor exposes its table/source and scope. Do not reuse a rating from an incompatible installation method.

If authorized standards tables are unavailable, provide a complete manual-data worksheet with provenance fields and exact missing-data diagnostics. This is a real calculation workflow, not a fabricated standards database. Dataset-backed automatic selection is enabled only for validated datasets.

13. PROTECTION, FAULTS, EARTHING AND CHECK STATUS

Assess the applicable relationship between design current, device rating/settings and corrected conductor capacity, together with conventional operating characteristics, fault duty and disconnection requirements where supported. Record the exact rule and its scope. A universal next-breaker-above-1.25×current rule is unacceptable as verified protective-device selection.

Protective-device records include manufacturer, model, poles, voltage, AC/DC applicability, characteristic/curve, rated/adjustable current, breaking capacity, source and compatible coordination data. Time-current plots use actual sourced curve envelopes and a logarithmic axis; guessed curves must not be labelled manufacturer data.

Provide worksheets for:
- Prospective short-circuit current from a stated supply/source impedance model.
- Minimum/maximum fault conditions where the model supports both.
- Loop impedance and disconnection-time checks for the selected earthing/ruleset.
- Conductor adiabatic thermal withstand using the entered/sourced material coefficient and applicable fault-energy/time model.
- Protective conductor/equipotential bonding assessment within a verified scope.
- RCD/RCBO selection criteria and compatibility records for the equipment and selected ruleset.
- Device breaking-capacity comparison and manufacturer-backed selectivity/backup coordination.

Require the necessary source impedance, fault level, earthing arrangement, cable path, protective-device data and operating conditions. Distinguish simplified source/fault estimates from a validated standards-based short-circuit study. Inverter and generator fault behavior must use supported source models or manufacturer data.

For current-limiting devices, use applicable manufacturer let-through I²t data at the relevant voltage and prospective fault current when comparing energy with conductor withstand. Do not substitute a generic trip-curve time or assume prospective current remains unchanged until clearing. State the temperature and time-range limits of the selected adiabatic model.

Report each check separately as passed, failed, warning, incomplete, not applicable or unsupported. Show the source, missing information and affected circuit. Removing required data must remove any prior pass. An RCD selection aid, worksheet or numerical model cannot certify an installation or replace required physical inspection/testing.

14. MOTORS, TRANSFORMERS, GENERATORS, INVERTERS AND UPS

Motor tools include electrical input/current, shaft power, efficiency, torque/speed, synchronous speed/slip, entered operating-point losses and start-sequence planning. Make rating basis, poles, frequency and operating conditions visible. Star/delta calculations apply only to explicitly compatible winding/rated-connection assumptions.

Start scenarios include DOL, star-delta, soft starter, VFD and user-defined methods, using entered or sourced current/PF/time characteristics. Account for loads already running and simultaneous/staggered starts. Do not substitute the running PF for starting PF by default or assign universal multipliers as manufacturer facts.

Transformer assessment includes kVA, current, utilization, entered impedance, no-load/load losses, selected temperature/derating assumptions and supported fault contribution. Generator assessment includes continuous/standby basis, kW/kVA limits, load steps, start demand and sourced derating. Predict voltage/frequency dip only if a validated model and necessary parameters are available.

Inverter and UPS assessment checks kW and kVA limits, supported phase configuration, overload duration, DC/AC limits, efficiency assumptions and usable backup energy. Runtime based on a constant-energy approximation must be labelled; use manufacturer discharge/efficiency curves for equipment-specific runtime claims.

Compare candidate equipment only from entered or verified sourced records. Show the limiting constraint and numerical headroom. Avoid one universal oversizing percentage. User-selected reserve capacity is a distinct design allowance, not an undocumented safety guarantee.

15. RENEWABLES, STORAGE AND BACKUP DISPATCH

Offer a clearly labelled daily-energy estimate and a separate chronological time-step simulation. Profiles may come from entered schedules, imported measurements or actual configured resource services. Record location, dataset/source, period, timezone and assumptions. Weather/resource availability is never simulated as a live connection.

PV features: module array description, installed DC capacity, orientation/tilt when a supported yield model uses them, inverter clipping, generation profile import, loss assumptions and self-consumption/export analysis. String checks use actual module/inverter datasheet ratings, temperature coefficients and design temperatures for cold Voc, hot Vmp, MPPT range and current limits. Yield estimates and equipment compatibility are separate checks.

Wind features: use supplied time-series output or a validated manufacturer power curve with appropriate wind inputs and declared losses. Do not infer real wind generation from an arbitrary decorative turbine animation.

Battery model: nominal/usable energy, chemistry/operating limits when known, initial SOC, lower/upper SOC, reserve, charge/discharge power, efficiencies, inverter coupling and optional supplied degradation assumptions. Declare whether capacity and power are on the AC or DC side. Apply each loss once.

Define whether SOC percentages refer to nominal energy or a specified usable window. Map minimum/maximum SOC, depth of discharge, usable capacity and reserve to one stored-energy state without applying the same restriction twice. Distinguish the absolute lower operating bound from energy reserved for critical loads.

Compute physical flows first: load served directly, battery charging/discharging, grid import/export, curtailment, losses and unserved critical demand. Maintain a per-step conservation equation and cumulative reconciliation. Respect power and energy limits, export/import constraints and available generation. Prevent simultaneous charge/discharge unless an explicitly justified model requires it.

Dispatch strategies include self-consumption, critical-load reserve, time-of-use scheduling and peak shaving. Show the objective, constraints and whether the strategy uses forecast information. A forecast-optimized result is not guaranteed real-world performance.

Outage simulation accepts start time/duration, critical-load groups, shedding priority and restart rules. Report served/unserved energy, load availability, limiting intervals and reserve exhaustion. Storage at zero/maximum SOC must behave correctly. Solar exported at noon must not erase evening grid imports merely because daily kWh totals match.

During an outage, grid-following generation is unavailable unless the project explicitly includes a compatible island-capable supply, supported transfer/isolation arrangement and operating mode that establishes the required voltage/frequency reference. Validate backup output, phase limits, restart and black-start capabilities against entered or manufacturer data. An installed PV array or charged battery does not by itself establish backup capability.

Start an outage from its stated initial energy state or the preceding simulated interval. Do not silently fill the battery at the outage boundary. If grid charging or precharging is permitted, include its actual power, timing, costs, losses and constraints in the scenario.

F04. 7.5 kW shaft-output motor, 400 V balanced three-phase, η=0.90, displacement PF=0.85 → 8.3333333333 kW electrical input and approximately 14.1507418919 A.
F05. Two compatible consuming sinusoidal loads at a common boundary, each 4 kW with +3 kvar and −3 kvar → 8 kW, 0 kvar and 8 kVA net complex-power magnitude; preserve their individual branch currents.
F06. 1 kW for 15 minutes → 0.25 kWh and 1 kW demand for that quarter-hour; if the other 45 minutes are zero, the complete hour averages 0.25 kW.
F07. Three equal unity-PF 10 A phase currents separated by 120° → approximately zero fundamental neutral current. One 10 A phase with the other two zero → 10 A neutral.
F08. Supplied resistance of 0.4 Ω in each of two conductors, 10 A DC or unity-PF AC under the declared resistive model → 8 V loop drop; no ampacity/compliance conclusion follows from this alone.
F09. A 10 kW consuming sinusoidal load corrected from displacement PF 0.80 to 0.95 → approximately 4.2131589482 kvar compensation. Do not automatically reduce its useful-load kWh.
F10. PV produces 1 kWh at noon and the load consumes 1 kWh in the evening, with no battery or other flows → 1 kWh physical export and 1 kWh physical import, billed separately.
F11. Battery stored energy starts at 5 kWh. Accept 2 kW AC charging for 1 hour at 90% charge efficiency → 6.8 kWh stored. Then supply 2 kW AC for 1 hour at 80% discharge efficiency → 4.3 kWh stored. Choose capacity/reserve bounds that permit both steps and do not apply the same conversion loss again.
F12. A 10 kWh nominal battery moves from SOC 80% down to a 20% lower bound, with 90% discharge efficiency and no other losses → 5.4 kWh deliverable AC energy, subject separately to its power limit.
F13. A constant 1 kW load integrated over an actual 23-hour/25-hour local DST day → 23/25 kWh respectively. Repeated local timestamps remain distinct by offset; test a complete leap-year February.
F14. Removing required cable/device/ruleset input turns the relevant check incomplete, invalidates stale pass status and identifies the missing input.
F15. A locked phase assignment remains unchanged after balancing. All proposed moves preserve topology and improve or honestly report the selected objective.
F16. Grid-tied-only PV without a supported island-capable supply cannot continue serving an isolated backup load merely because sunlight is present. An outage starts from the actual previous/stated battery state, without free precharging.
F17. An EV session with insufficient permitted charging time/power reports unmet energy and an infeasible deadline; it does not overrun phase/site constraints to force success.
F18. A malformed or unsupported import preserves the current project and edits. Export/import round trips preserve entity IDs, values, units, provenance and supported scenario relationships.
F19. Battery SOC, charge/discharge limits and interval energy balance hold at all boundaries. Summed interval energy agrees with cumulative plots and billing inputs within declared tolerances.
F20. Reports, charts, schedules, issue status and BOT explanations agree on one frozen project/scenario/engine revision. A delayed old result cannot overwrite a newer edit.

Add independent fixtures for every toolbox method, leading/lagging quadrants, L–L connections, zero/invalid inputs, unit conversion, fractional quantities only where allowed, tariff overlaps, negative prices where supported, all declared fault/thermal model boundaries and unavailable standards data. Physical-model claims need their own evidence beyond arithmetic fixtures.
